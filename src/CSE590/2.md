## Problem with naive beta reduction

$$
(\Lambda x.\Lambda y.x)yz \to (\Lambda y.y)z \to z
$$

This is suppose to return y, but it returns z. This is because the variable y is free in the body of the function, and it gets captured by the argument z.

Same problem in nameless representation:
$$
(\Lambda \Lambda 1)0 \to \Lambda \Lambda 0
$$
Which is supposed to be $\Lambda \Lambda 1$

### Alpha equivalence

$$
\Lambda x.\Lambda y.x = \Lambda a.\Lambda b.a
$$

variable swapping $e[x\lrArr y]$

$$
\begin{align}
z[x\lrarr y] = y if z=x, x if z=y, z otherwise\\
(\Lambda z.e)[x\lrarr y] = \Lambda z[x\lrarr y].e[x\lrarr y]
\end{align}
$$

$e=e'$ up to renaming of bound variables

$$
e_1e_1' = e_2e_2' := e_1 = e_2 || e_1' = e_2'
$$

$$
\Lambda x.e = \Lambda x'.e' := e[x\lrarr z] = e'[x'\lrarr z] \text{ for some} z \notin e,e',x,x'
$$

#### Free variables

$FV(x) = \{x\}$

$FV(e_1e_2) = FV(e_1) \union FV(e_2)$

$FV(\Lambda x.e) = FV(e)-x$

#### Example 

$$
\Lambda x.\Lambda x.x = \Lambda x.\Lambda y.y
$$

To prove this, we can define fresh variable z, and show that:
$$
(\Lambda x.x)[x\lrarr z] = (\Lambda y.y)[x\lrarr z]\\
\Lambda z.z = \Lambda y.y\\
z[z \lrarr a] = y[y \lrarr a]\\
a = a
$$

Thou shalt treat $\alpha $-equivalance terms as equal

### Capture-avoiding substitution

$$
(\Lambda x.e)e' -> e[x|-> e']
$$

#### Example

$$
(\Lambda x.\Lambda y.x)yz \to (\Lambda y.y)z \to z
$$

$((\Lambda y.x)[x |-> y])$ this is not allowed since y appeared

instead we do:

$$
\begin{align}
((\Lambda y.x)[x |-> y])z \to \\
((\Lambda a.x)[x |-> y])z \to \\
((\Lambda a.y)z) \to y 
\end{align}
$$
