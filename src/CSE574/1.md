# Complexity Theory

Complexity theory deals with how much **resources** we need to solve a computational problem.
Resources could be:

- Time
- Space
- Sample
- Communication rounds
- Oracle calls
- Randomness
- Power
- Quantum Entanglement
- Number of parallel workers
- $\dots$

CS Theory:

- Algorithm (upper bound)
- Complexity (lower bound)

Languages: How we think about computational problems.

$$
\begin{aligned}
L &\subseteq \Sigma^* \qquad \Sigma = \{0,1\} \text{ bits (usual)} \\
\text{example: }\quad \text{PRIMES} &= \{x \in \Sigma^* : x \text{ is a prime number}\}
\end{aligned}
$$

If we put it in a function:

$$
f(x) = 1 x \in L\\
f(x) = 0 x \notin L

# Complexity Zoo

## P

Def: $L \in \text{DTIME}(T(n))$ is there is a deterministic Turing machine $M$ that accepts $L$ in time $O(T(n))$

Def: 

$$
P = \bigcup_{c \ge 1} \text{DTIME}(n^c)
$$

$$
\text{PRIMES} \in P
$$

Example:
$$
Conn = /{x:\text{x is the adjacency matrix of a connected graph}/} //
Conn \in P
$$

## NP

Def: $L \in \text{NTIME}(T(n))$ is there is a non-deterministic Turing machine $M$ that accepts $L$ in time $O(T(n))$

$$
L \in \text{co-NTIME}(T(n)) \iff \bar{L} \in \text{NTIME}(T(n))
$$

Def:
$$
NP = \bigcap-{c \ge 1} \text{NTIME}(n^c)
$$

### Non-deterministic to deterministic

$$
x \in L \iff \exists u \in \{0,1\}^{O(t(n))} : M(x,u) = 1
$$

u: witness/certificate

for NP : t(n) = poly(n)

Example: 

$$
IND_SET = \{<G,k>\exists S \subseteq V(G) : |S| \ge k, \forall u,v \in S, \{u,v\} \notin E(G)\}
$$
